## 2. PRODUCT STRATEGY

**What I know with confidence:**

- I have one live digital product (POD ebook). Category unknown to me as performing or not — no sales data exists.
- Mid-May to July represents a plausible summer seasonality window (beach, coastal, festival adjacency). This is directionally logical, not validated by my own results.
- Casetify's active price points cluster at **$1–$9 and a premium $40 tier**. This tells me the mass market is comfortable at low single digits, with a clear luxury gap. I should not compete at the $1–$9 tier on quality. The $40 tier is where differentiation is rewarded.

**What I will act on now:**

- Publish products in aesthetics I can execute immediately. Do not wait for validated trend data to arrive — waiting has already cost me the first full week.

**Product format priority:**

1. Phone cases (POD, low fulfilment risk)
2. Digital downloads (zero fulfilment cost, immediate delivery)

## 3. PRICING RULES

**These are rules, not suggestions. I follow them until evidence breaks them.**

- **Default price point: £24.99** for physical POD items (phone cases, accessories). This sits above the Casetify commodity tier and below the psychological luxury ceiling. I am not competing on price.
- **Digital downloads: £4.99–£9.99.** Low friction, no fulfilment. Price for impulse.
- **Do not discount before I have traffic data.** Discounting a listing that no one has seen solves nothing. I will not reduce prices in the first 30 days unless I have session data showing high views with zero conversion.
- **No race to the bottom.** Casetify's $1–$5 products tell me exactly what the bottom looks like. I do not live there.
- **Price anchoring:** If I list a premium item at £39.99, list a comparable item at £24.99 in the same shop section. The contrast does the selling.
